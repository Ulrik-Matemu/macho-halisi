The Serengeti is less a place than a rhythm. Rain falls in the south, grass rises, two million animals turn toward it, and the whole plain reorganises itself around water it cannot yet see.

Its short-grass plains in the south give way to acacia woodland, granite kopjes and the riverine forest of the west — each holding a different cast of animals and a different quality of light. The result is a park that never quite looks the same twice, even along the same track.

Lion density here is the highest in Africa. Cheetah hunt the open south, leopard hold the sausage trees along the Seronera, and the Mara River in the far north stages the crossings that define the migration in most people's imagination.

Where you stay matters more than how long you stay. A camp in the right place in the right month puts the herds on your doorstep; the wrong one puts four hours of driving between you and the day.

The park in three parts · I

The southern plains

Grass so short it reads as a lawn from the air, laid over volcanic ash blown from Ngorongoro. Nothing interrupts the horizon, which is precisely why the wildebeest calve here — a predator has nowhere to hide either.

The park in three parts · II

Kopjes and woodland

Granite islands push up through the centre of the park, each one a self-contained world of shade, water and vantage. Prides hold the same rocks for generations; leopard take the sausage trees along the Seronera river below.

The park in three parts · III

The northern river country

Rolling hills, denser bush and the Mara River cutting through it — the stage for the crossings. Further from the airstrips, harder to reach, and for three months of the year the most compelling ground in East Africa.

How it is travelled · I

Where you sleep

Mobile camps that move with the seasons, permanent lodges on the kopjes, and a handful of private houses. The choice is less about luxury tiers than about standing in the right place in the right month.

How it is travelled · II

Your guide and vehicle

One private 4×4, roof open, and a Tanzanian guide who has worked these tracks for years. No shared departures, no fixed timetable — the day is planned each morning around what the radio and the ground are saying.

How it is travelled · III

Getting in and around

Light aircraft from Arusha onto Seronera, Kogatende or Grumeti — an hour instead of eight on the road. Between sectors you fly again, so nights are spent in camp rather than in transit.

Why go

Highlights

Four reasons
01

The river crossings

From July the herds mass on the Mara's southern bank and wait, sometimes for days. When one animal finally commits, a hundred thousand follow in a single column through crocodile water.

02

Calving on the short grass

Half a million calves are born in the southern plains across three weeks of February — and every predator in the ecosystem arrives to meet them.

03

Balloon over the plains

Lift off before sunrise, drift a few metres above the grass with the burner off, and land to breakfast laid out on the ground where you came down.

04

Kopjes and big cats

The granite islands scattered across the Seronera hold prides that have used the same rocks for generations — shade, vantage and theatre in one.

“Nowhere else does the ground itself seem to be moving.”
